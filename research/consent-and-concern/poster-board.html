Consent and Concern: Ethical Tensions in Contemporary AI Systems

A Two-Paper Investigation into AI Ethics, Control, and User-Constructed Constraint

Caquan Palmer Niagara University Sponsors: Dr. Glenn Pap · Dr. Taylan Sen · Dr. Joseph Little  |  Faculty Advisor: Dr. Barnwell

The Problem

  • The Dobrin–Crawford Collision: Two irreconcilable frames for AI ethics
  • Dobrin (2023): AI as collaborative tool. Risks managed through transparency, prompt discipline, and literacy.
  • Crawford (2021): AI as material empire. Built on extraction, exploited labor, and non-consensual harvesting.
  • User-side ethics cannot reach the parts of the system where the actual harm originates.
"Dobrin optimizes the symptom. Crawford names the disease."

Method

  • Diagnosis-Countermeasure Arc: Paper 1 documents the pipeline; Paper 2 builds MAE as response.
  • Extraction machinery and tiered-access economy convert public data into private capability.
  • Classification systems digitize discriminatory practices into "objective" systems.
  • Evidentiary risk escalates when AI hallucinations enter investigative and forensic pipelines.
  • Utility defense (consent kills capability) refuted through governance (EU AI Act) and technique.

Key Findings

Error Rates (Systematic & Concentrated)

  • Data Exposure: 4.2B records breached (IBM 2024)
  • Facial Recognition: 10–100× disparity for Asian/Black faces (NIST 2019)
  • Content Moderation: 78% false positive rate (Tech Policy Press 2025)
  • Hiring AI: 85.1% vs 8.6% bias (UW 2024)
  • Medical AI: 12–22% severe errors (Burns & Wilcox 2026)
  • Pricing Algorithms: 5.6–8.6 bp higher rates (UC Berkeley Haas 2018)

Pipeline

Extraction → Classification → Access → Evidentiary Risk

Three Ethical Tensions

  1. Consent vs. Utility — Systems want more data; users want boundaries. The binary is false.
  2. Control vs. Creativity — Contextually responsive output requires un-auditable internal reasoning.
  3. Reliability vs. Variability — Variability tracks existing inequalities, making it a distribution problem.
"AI is not failing. It is succeeding, at exactly the things its designers built it to do."

Conclusion

  • Designed, Not Broken: The harms are outcomes of systems doing what their design rewards.
  • AI extracts data at scale, classifies efficiently, decides quickly, and projects authority regardless of accuracy.
  • Users can remain passive participants or construct personal systems of constraint (MAE).
"The diagnosis is not pessimism. It is precision. Systems operating as designed can be redesigned."
