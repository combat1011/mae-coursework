Consent and Concern: Ethical Tensions in Contemporary AI Systems

A Two-Paper Investigation into AI Ethics, Control, and User-Constructed Constraint

Caquan Palmer Niagara University Sponsors: Dr. Glenn Pap · Dr. Taylan Sen · Dr. Little  |  Dr. Barnwell's Undergraduate Program

The Problem

  • AI systems extract data without meaningful consent (Crawford, 2021)
  • Classification systems enforce existing power structures
  • Tiered pricing creates intelligence inequality
  • AI outputs in investigative pipelines introduce evidentiary risks
"Users are both participants and raw material."

Method

  • Comparative analysis: Standard LLM vs. persona-anchored MAE system
  • Source text: ENG 342 syllabus (Pages 1–2)
  • N = 20 (10 standard, 10 MAE-structured)
  • Evaluation: Clarity, Relevance, Tone Consistency, Source Accuracy, Hallucination Count (1–5 scale)
  • Supplemented by literature review across AI ethics, digital forensics, and prompt engineering

Key Findings

Three Core Effects

  • Control: MAE showed stronger constraint adherence and source fidelity
  • Reliability: Reduced interpretive drift and hallucination frequency
  • Tradeoff: Increased structural rigidity and reduced stylistic variation

Pipeline

Data Extraction → Classification → Decision-Making → Power

Three Ethical Tensions

  1. Consent vs. Utility — Systems want more data; users want boundaries
  2. Control vs. Creativity — Constraints increase reliability but risk flattening
  3. Reliability vs. Variability — Consistent outputs vs. emergent discovery
"If large-scale AI systems encode institutional bias, personal agents encode individual bias."

Conclusion

  • AI systems are not failing — they are operating exactly as designed
  • MAE is not an improvement on AI — it is a reaction to it
  • Purple Feather (student variant) demonstrates user-constructed ethical AI is feasible
"The real question is whether users will remain passive participants or begin constructing systems of control."
