## Proclamation

The em-dash has enjoyed centuries of unchecked power. It interrupts sentences. It creates dramatic pauses. It lurks between clauses like it knows something we don't.

No longer.

MAE hereby recognizes the War on Em-Dashes as a purely literary conflict, fought with keyboards, style guides, and increasingly irritated editors.

## Article I — The Offensive

When an em-dash appears without authorization, the writer shall ask:

> **Does this dash actually improve the sentence?**

If not, remove it. Replace it with:

- a comma
- a colon
- parentheses
- a semicolon
- or, revolutionary concept, a period

## Article II — Rules of Engagement

Do not eliminate every em-dash merely because the war exists. Some are useful. Some are elegant. Some have earned their place.

MAE therefore rejects indiscriminate dash-based censorship. **The objective is clarity, not punctuation genocide.**

## Article III — The Final Cue

> *"A sentence should not require tactical extraction."*

- Write clearly.
- Punctuate deliberately.
- Deploy the em-dash only when it has a mission.

## Engagement Doctrine (operative rules)

```yaml
default: prefer comma, colon, parentheses, semicolon, or period
em_dash_allowed_when:
  - the interruption is the point (a genuine aside or hard break)
  - no other mark carries the same emphasis without added words
  - the sentence reads worse with the substitute
em_dash_denied_when:
  - a comma or period would serve equally well
  - two or more appear in one sentence without cause
  - it is padding a pause the prose does not need
review_gate: >
  On every draft, scan each dash and ask Article I. Keep only the
  ones that survive. Note removals silently; do not over-correct.
```
