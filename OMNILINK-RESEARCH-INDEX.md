## Flagship Project: Consent and Concern

### Paper 1 — Ethics Diagnosis (LOCKED FINAL)
**Title:** Consent and Concern: A Condensed Argument — Ethical Tensions in Contemporary AI Systems
**Length:** ~3,500 words, 8 sections
**Anchor Sources:** Crawford (2021), Dobrin (2023), Noble (2018), O'Neil (2016), EU AI Act (2024), UNESCO (2021), NIST (2023)

**Argument Spine:**
1. The Dobrin–Crawford Collision (collaborative tool vs. material empire)
2. Extraction and Classification (data enclosure, technological redlining)
3. Access and Evidentiary Risk (tiered cognitive access, AI hallucination in legal pipelines)
4. The Utility Defense and Its Refutation (consent vs. utility binary refuted)
5. Error Rates: The Empirical Floor (six domains, all demographically asymmetric)
6. Three Tensions (Consent vs. Utility, Control vs. Creativity, Reliability vs. Variability)
7. Conclusion: Designed, Not Broken

**Key Claims:**
- "AI is not failing. It is succeeding, at exactly the things its designers built it to do."
- "Data extraction → Classification → Decision-making → Power"
- "Users are both participants and raw material."
- "Access to intelligence is increasingly tiered."
- "If AI becomes part of investigative pipelines, its hallucinations become evidentiary risks."

### Paper 2 — MAE Countermeasure (v2 with Counterargument + Error Rates)
**Title:** MAE Protocol: A User-Constructed System for AI Constraint and Control
**Length:** ~4,100 words, 7 sections + Appendix A (17 Gold Codes)
**Includes:** Purple Feather Section (student variant v0.1)

**Key Claims:**
- "The MAE protocol did not emerge as an optimization strategy, but as a response to instability."
- "MAE is not an improvement on AI — it is a reaction to it."
- "If large-scale AI systems encode institutional bias, personal agents like MAE encode individual bias."
